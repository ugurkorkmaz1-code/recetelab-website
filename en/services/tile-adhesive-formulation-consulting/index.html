Formulation Is a Complete System

A tile adhesive is not developed by selecting an RDP and cellulose ether from a catalogue. Cement chemistry, aggregate grading, filler characteristics, polymer modification, water retention, rheology, water demand and production conditions interact with one another.

Cement + Aggregate + Filler + RDP + Cellulose Ether + Functional Additives + Water Demand + Production Process → Target Performance

Each component is evaluated as part of the complete formulation system. Changes in cement, mineral grading, polymer type, cellulose ether behaviour or process conditions can affect adhesion, open time, slip, workability, deformability and total product cost.

EN 12004-Oriented Performance Development

Development begins by defining the required product class, intended applications and measurable performance targets. The formulation and validation plan can then be structured around relevant EN 12004 requirements, including adhesion after conditioning, open time, slip and deformability where applicable.

ReçeteLab develops toward the agreed performance target and prepares the technical validation path. Formal classification or certification, where required, remains subject to testing by the appropriate laboratory or certification body.

Raw-Material Engineering & Local Adaptation

A formulation that performs with one cement, sand, filler, RDP or cellulose ether may not behave identically after a supplier or country change. We evaluate raw materials by function and behaviour, define suitable specifications and identify technically appropriate alternatives where possible.

This is especially important for manufacturers seeking to reduce dependence on imported materials, adapt a formulation to local supply or develop products for hot-climate markets.

Cost Engineering Without Blind Performance Loss

Cost optimization is treated as a system-level engineering task. Instead of simply reducing polymer or additive dosage, we review mineral packing, binder balance, water demand, additive efficiency, local alternatives and production stability together.

The objective is the required performance with an appropriate safety margin—not unnecessary over-performance that increases cost without creating commercial value.

From Laboratory to Industrial Production

Passing laboratory trials is only one stage. Industrial performance also depends on dosing accuracy, mixer geometry, fill level, mixing sequence, mixing time, low-dose additive distribution, raw-material moisture and plant temperature.

Our scale-up approach connects laboratory validation → pilot/first industrial batch → repeatability checks → production and QC limits.
